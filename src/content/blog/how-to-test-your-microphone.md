To test your microphone, select the input you want to use, allow microphone access, and record yourself speaking for 10 to 15 seconds. Stop the recording and listen through headphones. A moving input indicator confirms that audio is reaching the browser; playback helps you check whether your voice is clear.

You can use our [free online microphone test](/#microphone-test) for that recording, or start with your computer's sound settings. This guide explains both methods and what to check if the result is silent, quiet, noisy, or distorted.

Still connecting the hardware? Follow the [PC and Mac microphone setup guide](/blog/how-to-connect-set-up-microphone) first for USB connections, headset adapters, input settings and console compatibility.

## How to test your microphone online

The browser method lets you check a built-in laptop mic, webcam mic, USB microphone, or headset without installing another app. You need a supported browser, a secure HTTPS connection, and permission to access the microphone.

![Online microphone test in 4 steps: allow microphone access, speak normally, stop and listen back, then adjust and repeat](/blog/test-your-microphone/online-test-steps.svg "Allow access, speak, listen back, then change one setting at a time.")

1. **Connect your microphone.** Check the cable or wireless connection and turn off any physical mute switch. For an external mic, connect it before opening the test.
2. **Open the [microphone tester](/#microphone-test).** Choose your input from the device selector. If device names are unavailable before permission is granted, start with the system default and check the list after access is allowed.
3. **Select Start Free Microphone Test.** Approve the browser's microphone request. If you previously blocked the site, update the site's permission before trying again.
4. **Speak at your normal volume.** Say a short sentence and leave a brief pause. Watch for input activity, but do not try to force the indicator to a particular percentage.
5. **Stop recording and listen.** Use the playback controls to check both speech and the pause. Listen for understandable words, background noise, echo, crackling, and interruptions.
6. **Change one setting and repeat.** Adjust position or gain, then say the same sentence again. Download a clip if you need to compare results later.

For a repeatable sample, try: "This is my microphone check. I am speaking at the volume I will use during my next call." A consistent sentence makes changes easier to compare.

## How to test a mic on a Windows PC

Use Windows settings to check whether the operating system receives your microphone, then compare that result with a browser recording. These checks help separate a system input problem from a website or app permission problem.

### Windows 11 microphone test

1. Open **Start > Settings > System > Sound**.
2. Find **Input** and choose the microphone or headset you intend to use.
3. Open that input's properties. Where available, use **Start test**, speak, then stop the test and play the sample.
4. Adjust the input volume if needed, then compare another recording at the same speaking distance.

The controls vary with your Windows version and audio driver. Microsoft's [microphone setup and testing instructions](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/hardware/drivers/how-to-set-up-and-test-microphones-in-windows) describe the built-in test.

### Windows 10 and permission checks

Open **Settings > System > Sound**, select your input, and look for its microphone test or input-level indicator. If your version provides an indicator without playback, make a short browser recording to hear the result. For every Windows 10 menu and fix, see the [Windows 10 microphone test guide](/blog/how-to-test-microphone-windows-10).

If Windows receives sound but your browser does not, check the system's microphone privacy settings as well as the site's permission. Windows 11 places microphone access under **Privacy & security**; Windows 10 uses **Privacy**. Managed work or school devices may require an administrator to change access.

## How to check your microphone on a Mac

Open **Apple menu > System Settings > Sound > Input** and select your microphone. Speak normally and check the input response. Adjust input volume if that control is available for your device.

Then make a short browser recording so you can hear the signal. If access is blocked, check **System Settings > Privacy & Security > Microphone** and allow the browser. Follow any prompt to quit and reopen it after changing permission.

Apple's [sound input settings guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/change-the-sound-input-settings-mchlp2567/mac) explains input selection and volume controls. Menu names can differ on older macOS versions.

## How to test your mic on a phone or tablet

Open the browser tester on your Android phone, iPhone, or tablet and allow microphone access when prompted. Speak without covering the microphone openings, stop the recording, and listen back. A phone may use a different microphone depending on the connected headset and recording mode.

If a Bluetooth headset sounds muffled or is not offered as an input, disconnect it and compare the device's built-in microphone. Reconnect the headset afterward and check the active audio input again. Available inputs and recording formats depend on the browser and operating system.

On mobile, both browser-app permissions and website permissions can matter. A system voice-recording app can provide another comparison if browser access remains unavailable.

## What a successful microphone test should sound like

A useful result has understandable speech at a comfortable playback volume, without repeated dropouts or distracting distortion. Listen to the quiet pause too: it can reveal room noise or sound leaking from speakers.

![Microphone input level guide comparing a mic that is too quiet, a good level with headroom, and a level that is too loud and clipping](/blog/test-your-microphone/input-level-guide.svg "A good level peaks clearly but stays below the clipping point.")

If you are unsure whether your headphones or speakers are playing correctly, run the [left/right speaker and headphone test](/speaker-test) before judging the microphone recording.

- **The indicator responds and speech is clear:** the selected microphone reaches this browser. Check the same input in your meeting or recording app before relying on it there.
- **The indicator stays flat:** check mute, input selection, permissions, and the physical connection. A listed device is not proof that it is receiving sound.
- **The signal moves but playback seems silent:** check your output device, playback volume, and the audio player's controls. Test known audio through the same headphones to confirm that listening works.
- **Your voice is quiet:** move closer at a sensible distance and increase input gain gradually. Excessive gain can raise background noise too.
- **Your voice sounds harsh or breaks up:** lower input gain, avoid shouting directly into the mic, and compare another connection if the problem continues.

There is no universal ideal input percentage. The tester displays a relative digital level, not calibrated sound pressure in decibels. Its sample-rate display reflects the browser audio context, not a certificate of microphone quality. It does not measure end-to-end latency or automatically grade your sound.

## Why your microphone is not working

Work through these checks in order and repeat a short recording after each change. Changing several settings at once makes the cause harder to identify. For a complete walkthrough covering laptops, headsets, phones and echo, read [microphone not working? how to fix it](/blog/microphone-not-working).

### Access was denied or no permission prompt appears

Review microphone access in the browser's site settings and your operating system. In Chrome, microphone permissions are available under **Settings > Privacy and security > Site settings > Microphone**. Allow this site, then reload it. Google's [microphone permissions guide](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/2693767?hl=en) also covers default inputs and managed-browser restrictions.

Granting permission to one website does not grant it to every app. If the browser is controlled by your employer or school, ask the administrator about a blocked setting.

### The wrong microphone is selected

A computer may expose a laptop microphone, webcam microphone, USB input, and headset at the same time. Choose the device you plan to use in the tester and in your call app. After reconnecting equipment, check the choice again rather than assuming the previous selection still applies.

### The recording has echo or a high-pitched feedback sound

Use headphones and stop speaker playback during the test. Live monitoring routes your microphone back to your output; turn it off if you do not need it. A delayed echo can come from speaker audio entering the microphone, while room reflections can make speech sound distant even with monitoring disabled.

### The sound crackles, cuts out, or remains distorted

Try a direct USB connection instead of a hub, inspect detachable cables, and compare a different port where possible. Close other recording apps and retry. For audio interfaces, check the manufacturer's connection and driver instructions. A different browser or device can help determine whether the fault follows the microphone.

One failed test does not prove that hardware needs replacing. [More microphone troubleshooting answers](/faq) cover permissions, input selection, and recording limitations.

## Check your microphone before a meeting, game, or recording

After the browser test, select the same input in the app you actually plan to use. Check its mute button, input level, and any available test-call or recording feature. Applications can apply noise reduction, automatic gain, or other processing that changes the sound.

For a call, listen for intelligible speech rather than the loudest possible signal. For a voice recording, compare a spoken sentence and a short silent pause. For gaming with a headset, check the boom position and any physical mute control before joining voice chat.

Keep a consistent distance and change one setting at a time. See [what the microphone tester can and cannot measure](/features) before interpreting the result as a professional audio measurement.

## Save your test recording and understand privacy

The online tester processes audio in your browser and does not upload your test recording to a server. Stop the recording to release the microphone input. Download the clip before leaving if you want to keep it: refreshing, closing the page, discarding it, changing language, or starting a new test can remove access to the unsaved clip.

The browser chooses a supported recording format, and the download control shows the extension. This tool does not convert recordings to MP3. Website assets and fonts still load through the network; local audio processing does not mean that the whole website works offline. ## Frequently asked questions

### How can I test my microphone quickly?

Open the tester, select your microphone, allow access, and record a short sentence. Stop and listen through headphones. Watching an indicator checks incoming activity; listening to the recording helps you judge clarity.

### Can I test my microphone without installing software?

Yes. A supported browser can record and play a test without an extra installation. The tool needs JavaScript and microphone permission. If you prefer a system check, use your computer's existing sound settings.

### Why does my mic work here but not in Zoom, Teams, or Discord?

Each app can select a different input and have its own mute state, permission, or processing settings. Choose the tested device inside that app and use its own audio check where available.

### Does a moving microphone level mean my voice sounds good?

It confirms input activity, but the signal could include noise or distorted speech. Listen to a recording as well. If the player seems silent, check the playback device before changing the microphone.

### Can I check a headset, USB mic, or Bluetooth microphone?

Yes, if the browser and operating system expose it as an input. Connect it first, check the selector, and compare a recording. Wireless connection modes can affect which microphone is available and how it sounds.

### Can an online test tell me whether my microphone is broken?

Not by itself. Compare permissions, another input port, another app, or another device. If the fault follows the same microphone across working setups, consult its manufacturer's troubleshooting guidance.
